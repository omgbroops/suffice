The live change satisfies the reported capacity problem without requiring a particular data structure. Each drain now allocates and sorts all W active weight keys, including ineligible keys, costing O(W log W) plus waiter work. Draining repeats after each queued acquisition. Many distinct waiting weights at small capacity may therefore cost more than before; this workload was not measured. This is a documented tradeoff, not a demonstrated violation of the public issue. Removing resolved entries, including the old all-eligible branch that left resolved promises in a bucket, avoids retaining inactive buckets. Native Promise resolution runs callbacks after synchronous draining, so deleting these resolved buckets does not introduce callback reentrancy during traversal.
