# Suffice versus stock Pi versus native Codex: Sol low and xhigh

The six-way comparison completed on Sonion with up to six concurrent isolated actors. There are 30 valid attempts across five fresh historical issues, plus six quarantined attempts from an invalid contract. All 36 attempts retain usage and results. Known API-equivalent inference totals $3.597710, including $0.434644 for the invalid task; no full attempt costs are unknown. These are not subscription cash charges and exclude the engineering parent's inference.

Including each configuration's invalid-contract attempt as campaign overhead raises these costs per valid acceptance to $0.06116, $0.07579, $0.12307, $0.17739, $0.19863 and $0.26339 respectively. The invalid task is excluded from comparative task accuracy, not from the complete expenditure ledger.

Observed mean savings: Suffice versus Codex is 51.4% at low and 35.3% at xhigh. Suffice versus stock Pi is 16.3% at low and 16.6% at xhigh. Suffice low versus Codex xhigh is 77.0% cheaper, with the same four accepted issues. This cross-effort comparison combines harness and effort effects. It does not establish a pure harness effect. Suffice xhigh cost about 2.82 times Suffice low without rescuing the failed task in this sample. Suffice was cheaper than Codex on all five pairs at each effort. Against Pi, Suffice won four of five cost pairs at low but only two of five at xhigh; most of its xhigh mean advantage over Pi comes from the first issue. Its xhigh median is slightly worse than Pi. These percentages describe this cohort; they are not population superiority estimates.

All six configurations solved the cancellation-startup bug in p-queue #256, synchronous cache failure in p-memoize #62, abort-listener cleanup in p-retry #71 and overlapping debounce cancellation in p-debounce #34. All six passed original suites but failed the independent timer-drift reproduction for p-throttle #49. Every accepted patch is also budget eligible under the common $2 / 600-second allowance. No actor operational failures or unknown inference costs remain. Patch-collection failures are evaluator incidents, recorded separately.

The shared failure is concrete. All six throttling patches changed the scheduled execution timestamp to `Math.max(now, oldestTimestamp + interval)`; some also removed a redundant clamp. This addresses expired scheduling timestamps but leaves the timer-drift scenario unsolved. Relevant scheduling code was retrieved and edited. The evidence supports an incomplete behavioral hypothesis, not a simple failure to locate the file. It cannot establish the model's internal reasoning. More effort repeated essentially the same repair. No new verification mechanism has been implemented on the strength of this one example.

I made a contract-selection error in n06: GitHub #100 was a PR whose public text expressly preserved fractional retry values, while the selected reference commit and private check rejected them. Baseline-fail/reference-pass controls alone did not establish a faithful public contract. All six n06 grades are quarantined and must not be interpreted as capability failures. The replacement actual issue, p-debounce #34, was explicitly reviewed and controlled before its six actors started. The original protocol, raw results, costs and amendment remain available. Replacement selection occurred after earlier outcomes were visible, so this remains exploratory. CONTRACT-AMENDMENT.json records the exact decision; TASK-CONTRACTS.initial.json retains the initial mistaken review.

The patch collector also encountered a Unix socket created by p-memoize's native tsimp test runner. Dispatch paused. A separate evaluator-only recovery captured regular files, omitted runtime cache/socket data, and graded the existing six patches. No models were rerun, no actor received hidden feedback, and the frozen harness and campaign inputs were unchanged. PREPARATION-NOTES.md preserves setup corrections, rejected candidates and recovery details.

Both Suffice efforts retained frozen general-v1 behavior; only the previously authorized effort configuration differs. Native Pi 1.2.0 kept its own prompt and default read/bash/edit/write tools. Native Codex 0.163.0 kept its native prompts, tools, execution and compaction. Observers recorded costs and enforced common limits. All configuration identities, receipt totals and component costs reconcile. INTEGRITY.json verifies original general-v1 inputs, frozen campaign sources and expected effort runtime bytes. The Pi observer's effort guard is explicitly accounted for.

Limitations: five small asynchronous libraries, one attempt per issue/configuration, common model and correlated failures, several public solution hints, one parent contract reviewer rather than a second human, and a post-selection replacement. These are stateful repairs, not long-horizon or large integration tasks. Configuration submission order rotated within phases but was not fully counterbalanced across validation/recovery phases. Shared account/backend caches and concurrent contention were uncontrolled; latency is descriptive. Mean actor times were 63/73/70 seconds for Suffice/Pi/Codex low and 164/213/139 seconds at xhigh. Grading time is excluded from actor latency. This cohort does not establish that low effort matches xhigh on harder tasks or that Suffice generally beats Codex. It is not the Coding Agent Index.

The smallest justified decision is to retain Sol low as the cost-efficient baseline for this task class and retain all three native comparison adapters. Stock Pi explains part of the efficiency advantage, and Suffice adds a smaller observed advantage over Pi here. Keep the throttling failure as a development example for diagnosing missing behavioral evidence. Before adding any harness mechanism or declaring broad superiority, repeat the comparison on more diverse independently reviewed issues. No further campaign or optimization was launched automatically.
